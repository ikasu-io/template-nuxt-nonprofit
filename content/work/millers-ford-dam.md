The Miller's Ford dam was built in 1923 to power a feed mill that closed
in 1958. For the next sixty years it did nothing except hold back silt,
warm the water behind it, and stop smallmouth bass and darters from
swimming upstream to spawn. It was also dangerous: two people drowned in
the churning water below it in the 1990s.

We started talking with the township, which owned it, in 2022. Taking out
a dam takes longer than people expect. There were sediment tests,
engineering drawings, a state permit, a federal permit, and a lot of
kitchen-table conversations with the neighbors who had fished above it
all their lives.

## What we did

In August 2026 an excavator broke the dam into pieces over eleven days.
The silt that had built up behind it, tested clean, was spread on the
banks and planted with switchgrass, river birch and buttonbush. By the
first rain, the creek had found its old channel.

## What happens next

We will survey fish and insects above the old dam every spring for five
years. The first count, in September, already found darters a mile
upstream where nobody had seen one since anyone could remember.
