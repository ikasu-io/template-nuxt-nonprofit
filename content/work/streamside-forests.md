A creek with no trees along it heats up in the summer sun, and warm water
holds less oxygen. Its banks crumble, and soil and fertilizer from the
fields wash straight in. A strip of forest thirty to a hundred feet wide
fixes most of that within ten years.

We work with landowners who are willing to give up that strip. We bring
the trees, the tubes, the volunteers and the paperwork for the county's
cost-share program; they keep the cattle out and let the trees grow.

## What we plant

Sycamore, silver maple, river birch, black willow, swamp white oak and
buttonbush close to the water; hackberry, redbud and pawpaw further up
the bank. All of them grow wild along the Sallow already, and all come
from a nursery two counties over.

## The Tanner Creek campaign

Tanner Creek has one bare mile left, along the Kessler and Moyer farms.
This fall and next spring we want to plant ten thousand trees there.
