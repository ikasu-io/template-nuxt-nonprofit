You usually hear a belted kingfisher before you see it: a dry rattle,
like someone running a stick along a fence, and then a blue-gray streak
low over the water.

In June, a student from Wren Hollow Elementary heard one at our
water-testing site on Tanner Creek. Two weeks later Tom Kessler found the
nest, a tunnel in the cut bank below the trees we planted on his land in
2014.

> "Twelve years ago that bank was bare dirt and cows. Now there's a bird living in it."

## Why it matters

Kingfishers eat small fish, and small fish need cool, clear water with
insects to eat. A kingfisher nesting on a creek is a sign that the whole
chain underneath it is working again.

The trees did it. They shade the water, so it stays cool enough in
August. Their roots hold the bank, so the water runs clearer. Their
leaves fall in, and the insects eat them.

## Come and see

The nest is on private land, so please do not go looking for it. But the
kingfishers fish the whole stretch, and you have a good chance of seeing
one at the Miller's Ford access early in the morning.
