Every month since 2012, a class from one of eleven schools has stood in
the Sallow and measured it. This spring, students from the Hollow County
Career Center put all of it together: 7,412 samples from twenty sites.

Here is what they found.

## The river is getting warmer

Summer water temperatures at Tanner Creek have gone up by about two
degrees since 2012. On the stretches where we planted trees, they have
gone down. The students' chart is now the first thing we show landowners.

## Nitrate still spikes after rain

After every heavy spring rain, nitrate from fertilizer jumps for about
three days, mostly below the bare stretches of Tanner Creek. Streamside
forests catch some of it before it reaches the water.

> "We thought the chemistry would be the interesting part. It turned out the bugs told us more."

## The bugs are coming back

The count of mayflies, stoneflies and caddisflies, which only live in
clean water, has gone up at fourteen of the twenty sites. The biggest
jump is at Miller's Ford, below the old dam.
