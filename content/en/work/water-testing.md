Once a month, a class pulls on waders and walks into the river. They
measure the temperature, oxygen, nitrate and how cloudy the water is, and
they kick-net a square yard of riverbed to count the insects living in
it. Mayflies, stoneflies and caddisflies only live in clean water, so
the count says more than any single chemical test.

Every result goes into a shared spreadsheet that anyone can open. Over
fourteen years it has shown the river warming at Tanner Creek, the
nitrate spike after every spring rain, and, since the dam came out, more
mayflies at Miller's Ford.

## For teachers

We bring the kits, the waders and a staff member for the first three
visits. After that, most classes run it themselves. The program fits
the Ohio science standards for grades 4 to 12.
